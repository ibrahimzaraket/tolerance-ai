Exercise 1

Estimating Forecast Turnover

Identify and evaluate the different adapted methods to estimate the forecast turnover of your project, and specify the modalities and reasoning behind your choices. Source: Workshop 4 – Building the Business Plan

📦
Offer Approach
Competitive / "Analog" Method
Estimation of the potential commercial turnover from competitor figures published by competitors or obtained through direct questioning of companies.
📂 Commercial Court Registry (CR & balance sheet), société.com, direct inquiry
⚠ For a new activity: no reference figure available
"Reference" / Accounting Sector Statistics Method
Estimation of the potential turnover based on accounting sector statistics, taking into account the economic environment of the future company, the range of products sold, the target clientele, limits to the activity, and the resources implemented (hours or days billed).
📂 Approved management centers, professional organisations
🎯
Demand-Driven Approach
"Purchase Intentions" Method
Estimation of the potential turnover based on data collected in the field survey: quantities usually purchased, frequency of purchase, psychological price, average basket, etc.
📂 Field survey
⚠ Discrepancy between expressed purchase intentions and actual purchases — generally a very high WTP assumption; treat with great caution
"Targets and Market Share" Method
Estimation of potential turnover by applying the company's desired or anticipated market share, which depends on: unconquered potential customers ("relative non-consumers") and competitive intensity.
📂 Market study, quantitative inquiry
⚠ Based on demand, take-up and penetration rates — consider with great caution
🔀
Alternative Approaches
"Break-Even Point" Method
Estimate of the potential turnover for which one would make neither profit nor losses — the one which just covers fixed and variable costs.
📂 Approved management centers, professional organisations, BM & resources to be implemented
⚠ Sets a target point but does not predict the turnover that will actually be achieved
Testing & Experimentation Method
Estimate of the potential turnover based on real-life situations: on a test market, online, or within a supporting structure (salary portage, business project support contract) or even as a micro-entrepreneur.
📂 Market (test or online), supporting structure
⚠ Difficult to transpose or extrapolate the turnover obtained under these particular conditions to full activity
📊
Synthesis
  • These approaches are complementary — no single method is sufficient on its own.
  • They allow for the establishment of the greatest possible number of scenarios.
  • Supply-based estimation < Demand-based estimation (maximum high hypothesis): demand-driven methods tend to overestimate.
  • Combining multiple methods cross-validates projections and reduces the risk of systematic bias.
✅
Modalities of Choice
Use the Offer Approach first — Start with competitor and sector statistics data (société.com, Commercial Court Registry, professional organisations) to anchor a realistic baseline. This is grounded, verifiable, and directly comparable to the market.
Cross-validate with the Demand Approach — Conduct a field survey to gather purchase intentions and estimate a market share scenario. Apply a conservative discount (typically 30–50%) to purchase intention data, as stated WTP systematically exceeds actual spend.
Set a floor with the Break-Even Method — Calculate the minimum turnover needed to cover all fixed and variable costs. This defines the viability threshold and ensures projections are realistic relative to cost structure.
Validate with real-life testing if possible — A pilot run (online channel, test market, or micro-entrepreneur status) provides actual revenue data before committing to full operations. Results must be extrapolated with caution.
Build at least three scenarios — Pessimistic (offer-based floor), Realistic (adjusted demand estimate), and Optimistic (target market share). This range communicates financial risk and resilience to investors and stakeholders.
Key principle: No single method provides the definitive forecast. The strength of the business plan lies in the convergence of several complementary methods — the closer they align, the more credible the projected turnover.
